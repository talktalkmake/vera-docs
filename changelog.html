Changelog ​

New in VERA, week by week: what you can now do, and how it helps. The newest week is first, and weeks with nothing new are left out. Link to a single week with its anchor, for example changelog.html#week-36.

Week 38 · Oct 26, 2026 ​

Owners ​

  • You can name anyone to your assistant app, vendors included, and it finds them in VERA, so you can say "use Talia for the design" without looking anyone up.
  • You can ask your assistant app what to charge for a proposal at your target LER, or how many hours a client's budget buys, and it answers with VERA's own figures, fixed fees included, so the price you quote is right first time.
  • You can see whether each person you name on a proposal has the time, not just their whole discipline, and the earliest week the work could finish, so you don't promise a date one busy person can't meet.

Week 37 · Oct 19, 2026 ​

Owners ​

  • Your contribution margin now counts all your delivery team's pay, billable or not, the way Greg Crabtree measures it, so the number reflects time nobody billed; it leads the Money tile on your home page.
  • You can see how well your pay turns into profit the way Greg Crabtree measures it, with direct, management and total LER and contribution margin for the last 12 months, and project LER and utilization showing why direct LER moved, so you know whether to raise prices or fill people's time.
  • You can connect your own assistant app to VERA and have it draft proposals for you: it reads your past projects, people and costs, finds the earliest start your team can staff, and leaves a draft in VERA for you to review, so a first proposal takes minutes.
  • You can set your company's headquarters in Settings, so salary bands can be refreshed from your region's wage data without asking us.
  • You can see everyone's hours week by week on Contributions, and each person's on their own page, so a short project or a busy fortnight shows up without waiting for the month to end.
  • You can see what each task on an ongoing internal project has cost, beside its hours, so you know where that overhead goes.
  • You can bring your Harvest history in one project at a time, with a progress bar, and stop and come back days later without losing an answer, so a long history can be reviewed over several sittings.
  • You can give an evergreen internal project a monthly hours budget, so you can see each month whether it is taking more time than you meant it to.

Project managers ​

  • You can see the hours behind every percentage on the capacity heatmap, committed against available, so you can tell a part-timer's 50% from a full-timer's.
  • You can see on a new project's Financials card what it still needs, such as a billing rate, an hours budget or an allocation, so you can set it up without guessing.
  • You can rename the projects you manage, so a new name takes effect without asking an owner.
  • You can see how a person's week stands at a glance, done, planned and free against their capacity, with today marked, so it is clear who has room and who is already full.
  • You can switch your project list to all projects, so you can find and help with a colleague's project while they're away.
  • You can hand a project you run to another manager from Edit project, so someone can cover it while you're away.
  • You can see each month's billable and non-billable hours by project and person on the Hours tab, and download them, so preparing invoices takes one export.
  • You can trust Capacity, your home page's People tile and the morning summary to count hours already recorded this week, so they agree with each person's own page all week long.

Everyone ​

  • You get a morning edition each working day, with what you recorded yesterday and how this week's plan is going on each of your allocations, so you can catch a missed day and see where your week stands before you start.

  • You can see, on each of your allocations, how many hours are planned for you this week and how many you've recorded, with one line saying whether you're ahead, on track or behind for the week, so you can keep your time up to date as you go.

  • You can see what your allocations ask of you each week against your normal week, so you know the plan fits the hours you work.

  • You can tell at a glance which internal projects run indefinitely and which have a deadline and budget, because the Internal view of the projects list now shows each one's kind, and every list that names a client puts the client first.

  • You can reach your clients from the Projects page, on a Clients tab beside your projects, so the sidebar is one link shorter.

Week 36 · Oct 12, 2026 ​

Owners ​

  • You see each project's money as today and at finish, side by side on one scale, so a project heading for a loss stands out while there is still time to act.
  • You can make an internal project evergreen or measured, so Admin-style work runs indefinitely with no deadline, while an internal build gets a deadline, a budget and, when you outsource it, a vendor or a contractor on a fixed price.
  • When you add a person, VERA emails them an invite to sign in, so they know they have access and how to get in; a Send invite button reaches anyone you added earlier.
  • You can bring your Harvest history into VERA from the console, check it before it lands, undo it in one click, and top up time recorded since, so you switch without losing your past.
  • You are warned when someone holding an allocation has no cost rate, so no project's margin looks better than it is.
  • You see a progress card while VERA reads your Harvest account, and you can leave the page and come back while it works.
  • You read LER as gross margin over direct labor, with contractor and vendor fixed fees treated alike, so it shows how well your own people's time is priced.
  • You see what an evergreen project, and PTO, Sick and Leave, cost in total, by month and by person, so ongoing overhead has a price.

Project managers ​

  • You can attach task links, ticket numbers or notes to an envelope, so everyone knows which work counts toward it.
  • You can see a month's hours by project and by person on Staffing and download them as a CSV, so month-end checks on retainers and capped contractors take minutes.
  • You can record when a vendor is away, so your plans show a partner's absence before it becomes a surprise.
  • You can move a project to another client, see its client, type and deadline at the top of its page, and see how well your deadlines are held.
  • You can correct a project's hours budget yourself after it is created, without waiting for an owner.
  • You can give a project the same name as one under another client, so each client's projects keep the names they really use.
  • You can see an evergreen project's hours by month, by task and by person, so you know where ongoing work like Admin or PTO goes.

Everyone ​

  • You can change which allocation a time entry counts toward when you edit it, so every hour lands on the allocation it belongs to.

Week 35 · Oct 5, 2026 ​

Owners ​

  • You can pay a contractor a fixed price through a liability that builds up evenly across its dates and stays paired with their envelope, so fixed-fee work earns and costs on the same clock.

Project managers ​

  • You can edit a retainer period on its own page and attach vendor liabilities to it, so work you farm out each month is planned with the period.
  • You have a Vendors tab on Staffing listing every vendor and contractor with their commitments, so outside work is as visible as your team's.
  • You can resolve or dismiss several unresolved items at once, so a long queue clears in a few clicks.
  • Your envelope charts show each week's number, and a retainer's burn-up runs on through the periods you have planned, so you can look further ahead.
  • You can edit any time entry on a project you manage, so you can put mistakes right without asking an owner.

Week 34 · Sep 28, 2026 ​

Owners ​

  • You can convert a project from one type to another, keeping its history, when an engagement changes shape.

Project managers ​

  • Your home page opens with a one-screen brief of money, projects and people, and your morning message carries the same brief, so you start the day knowing where to look.
  • You see a renewal due on a retainer's own page, so no engagement runs out without a next period.
  • You can duplicate an envelope and see the time recorded outside envelopes, so staffing a project is quicker and no hours go unaccounted for.
  • Your Needs you page lists everything waiting on you, including projects that still need setup, so nothing slips between queues.

Everyone ​

  • You find the console organized into hubs (Money, Staffing, Needs you, Performance, Settings and Logs), each with tabs, so related pages sit together.

Project managers ​

  • You can run retainers: each period has its own page for staffing, edits and tasks, and Re-up rolls a retainer forward from its last terms.
  • You are told when a period on your retainer is about to end with no successor, so you can renew in time.
  • You find a project's configuration, PM and enabled tasks on one card, and advice links open the form they point to, so setup takes fewer clicks.

  • You can run VERA without Slack, sign in with Google, and move your own account between owner and admin.

Project managers ​

  • You get a "What this project needs" card listing each project's next steps in plain language, ranked by what clears the most.
  • You can set a project's start date, so its schedule, charts and plans are measured from the day work really began.
  • You can create projects and clients, and make yourself the PM of a project that has none, so you can get work moving without waiting for an owner.
