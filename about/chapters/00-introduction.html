The Vision ​

What are we doing here? ​

You're here because you want a better way to run your business, and you hope the answers may be enclosed here give me a few minutes of your attention, and I will describe a world you can achieve by following some simple (but not easy) steps.

You run a small company that uses a lot of people's time in delivering high-quality services to your clients. Your leadership team finds it easy to communicate with each other when difficult moments happen. People's heart rates may increase, but they believe each other when they say they have the company's best interests at heart. They are able to have adult conversations without ad hominem friction. Your team prioritizes decision making at the lowest possible level in the company, and you as the owner are not the bottleneck. It's common for you to sit back and watch passionate people fight for what they think is the best idea and how to go about accomplishing hard things without turning to you and asking what you think we should do. When decisions get made, you don't need to be the tie-breaker, and you also don't need to go around the company convincing people that weren't in the room that you want this change to be rolled out. Things just have a way of getting done without your inclusion.

Because your company is small, you often have to jump into different seats in order to get things over the line, and besides, you enjoy the flexibility this offers. A change is as good as a rest.

When you do pick up some account management work for an important client, you do so because you know the person on the client side well and you have a good working relationship. When you hear that some important piece of work is going to be late for this client, your project manager responsible for delivering that work does not treat you differently than any other account manager. When you're operating as the account manager, they treat you not as the owner but as the person responsible for managing the client relationship. People don't pander to your needs and your opinions just because you are in the place. They treat you as an equal when you are an equal working in the team. If you have a bad idea, you can trust your team to tell you there's a better way.

When things do go wrong or are late, everyone knows what's important and what we must do in order to both correct that situation and avoid it from happening again in the future. This is not one of those workplaces where our website says that we're always creatively daring, but the work that we output most of the time is more profitable than polished. Your employees don't gossip about how we say one thing and do another. You're known as a company, internally and externally, that keeps things simple and operates smoothly because everyone knows what to do.

Employees know where they stand. Roles, bands, and salaries are normalized in the hiring process, so by the time someone is approaching their first year working for you, they know full well where they stand in terms of the salary they get and the band they live within for their given role. You never have to worry again about someone knocking on your door and asking for more money because they need it. You communicate in advance what is required to get to the next level and the accompanying increase in salary, which they know up front everyone in the company understands whether the company pays above, below, or at market rates, and they also understand that a great place to work, where learning is prioritized, is its own form of value. People know that when they do leave for more money or greener pastures, they will be better at the job that they did when they arrived. They trust that when you say people get treated well, they actually do. This is why people work hard in your company to enable profit. They understand the story of the company, the risks that you took and continue to take as owners, and are appreciative that you do the best you can to make this a good place to work and prioritize their career as much as possible. They accept that this business is here to make profit for the ownership group.

You understand and accept that at some point your best people will no longer work for you. You feel completely comfortable in job interviews, telling the candidate that at some point they will come to you and say that they have a better offer elsewhere. You describe to them a few years of career acceleration, psychological safety, and high standards that excite the right kind of people. When they accept your offer (which fits within the salary bands your salary cap defines), they know that you have a logic which is fair and which makes the company financially resilient. Employees understand that there are likely to be people above them and below them in their discipline. A project manager knows that there may be a higher-level project manager than them and possibly one below. A designer understands, when they're hired as a level one, that the level two designer gets given more responsibility and more money, and that at the level four designer has bigger expectations and a larger salary than both the Level One and Level Two designer.

Everyone understands no one micromanages. When tasks are delegated down, they're well described and easy to understand. Relationships between people in the company come with golden rules so that subordinates can understand, in simple terms, what good looks and feels like. These golden rules are in alignment with the operating values of the company. Each project manager understands that the head of project management always wants emails to be sent to clients within two hours of receiving a request. The head of project management has made this clear to their project managers, and each of them understands and follows this golden rule without passive aggression or uncertainty in their day-to-day work. People avoid assumptions and make things clear to each other without friction or delay.

Every day has the same structure. There was a brief morning meeting for people to confirm the commitments of the day and to perform last-minute pre-flight checks so we can avoid delays or errors with the very latest information at hand. These meetings run smoothly because we perform them every day and we always avoid wandering off course. People understand that the commitments they make each day to get work done are to themselves, to their team, and to clients. At the end of each work day, we measure progress in order to respect the commitment to ourselves, our team, and our clients. People know that when they say they're going to get work done by the end of the day, that's the expectation for themselves, from their team, and from their clients. No one at the end of each day is sat wondering where work is.

When your team makes mistakes, you find out because they raise their hand as soon as they realize what's happened. People come to their colleagues to share bad news quickly. You have a process to diagnose what kind of mistake it was and if it's one we need to avoid from happening again or if it's one we can learn from. Your stress levels are kept low because people volunteer having made mistakes. Your team has a process to follow when they're made, and nobody panics. You trust your team to make good decisions, and they trust you not to need to get involved just because you're the boss. Every time a mistake happens, your junior people learn quickly from your senior people staying calm and quickly managing situations, producing conclusions that have the least harmful impact and the biggest long-term benefit for improved decision making

Every single project you sell and deliver comes with the expectation that it needs to win on its own terms. Everyone in the company understands that the labor efficiency ratio for every single project needs to be at a certain level. Decisions are made with or without you in the room that benefit the prioritization of LER, balanced with the need to do no harm to the client, the relationship, or the value created by the project. You have a company that's profitable because every project is profitable.

Owners of other agencies frequently find ways of floating the idea of acquiring your company. They hear great things from both the client side and the employee side about how your operation is run. You always seem to find a way to leave both parties happy, and from what you've shared with them, your numbers are impressive. You're able to see yourself in two possible futures:

  1. One where you sell your asset and recognize the sweat equity and intellectual property you've built over long years
  2. The other where you continue to build your asset so that you can exit your role but retain ownership Both options are viable because you have prioritized running a saleable Company. ß
